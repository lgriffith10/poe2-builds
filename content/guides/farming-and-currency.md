Once your build is stable in maps, the question becomes what to do with them. This guide covers what each mechanic gives, how to choose, and how to turn loot into currency. If you have not finished the Precursor Fortress yet, start with [Starting the Endgame](/guides/endgame-start#picking-a-league-mechanic).

> **In short.** Pick one or two mechanics your build is good at, add them to your maps with Tablets and Atlas passives, and sell everything that has value instead of letting it sit in your stash. Most income comes from ordinary drops sold in bulk, not from lucky Exalted Orbs.

## What Each Mechanic Gives

| Mechanic | Main rewards | Pinnacle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Breach** | Catalysts (jewellery quality), Breach rings (catalysable to 40% instead of 20%), Breach Splinters, and Hiveblood and Wombgifts to craft jewellery and currency at the Genesis Tree | Xesht, We That Are One, through Breach Splinters; the Breachlords Tul and Esh through Breachstones |
| **Ritual** | Tribute to buy Omens and uniques. Omens change how crafting currency works (an Omen of Greater Exaltation makes the next Exalted Orb add two modifiers) and sell well | The King in the Mist, then The Bodach |
| **Delirium** | Distilled Emotions to instill amulets, jewels and Waystones, and Simulacrum waves | Tang'Mazu, through the Simulacrum |
| **Expedition** | Runes, Verisium (Runeforging and Ward) and Kalguuran skills; Logbooks to explore the ocean | Olroth, then the meteor encounter |
| **Abyss** | Abyssal Troves full of loot, including bones for desecration crafting | The Lightless bosses, then the Vessel of Kulemak |
| **Incursion (Vaal Temple)** | Temple rooms: double corruption, Soul Cores, special affixes on Vaal uniques, mechanical limbs | Atziri, the Red Queen |
| **Trial of the Sekhemas** | Relics, jewels and Time-Lost Jewels (exclusive to this trial) | Zarokh, the Temporal |
| **Trial of Chaos** | Chaos and Vaal Orbs from every monster, corrupted items, Soul Cores from boss rounds | The Trialmaster, with the three Fates |

Smaller encounters add to every map: Strongboxes (craftable for more loot), Essence monsters (each Essence guarantees a modifier), Shrines, corrupted areas whose cleansed maps can drop Fracturing Orbs, and Azmeri wisps whose possessed monsters drop Talismans.

> **Which one suits my build?** Every build guide on this site ranks the eight mechanics below for that build, in its **Endgame Farming** section, with the reasons behind each place.

## Mechanic by Mechanic

For each mechanic: how it plays, what its loot is for, how to turn it into Exalted and Divine Orbs, and what kind of build does it best.

### Breach

**How it works.** Walk over a Breach hand in a map to open an unstable Breach: Hiveborn pour out in waves, and killing enough of them before the timer runs out stabilises it. A group of Rare monsters, sometimes the Marshals of Xesht, then has to die to close it, and drops a large pile of loot. **Hives** are a variant where you defend Ailith while she closes the Breach (run through the walls around her first to break them). The hub is the Keepers of the Flame Monastery, south on the Atlas.

**What you get and what it is for.**

- **Catalysts** raise the quality of rings and amulets, which strengthens their modifiers of the matching type: catalysing a life ring gives more life. Breach rings take up to 40% quality instead of 20%. This patch also added jewel Catalysts (Ruby for fire, Quartz for caster, Tourmaline for attribute and Mephrite for defence modifiers).
- **Hiveblood and Wombgifts** are spent at the **Genesis Tree**: Hiveblood hatches Wombgifts into rings, amulets, belts or currency. Points in the tree let you steer what hatches: the modifier type (minion, caster…), a minimum modifier level, or the base type, including bases found nowhere else (Lament, Portent and Absent) with implicits such as maximum elemental resistances or increased maximum life on rings.
- **Breach Splinters** open the Realmgate to the pinnacle boss Xesht, whose drops include [[Skin of the Loyal]], [[Xoph's Blood]], [[Choir of the Storm]], [[The Pandemonius]], [[Hand of Wisdom and Action]] and [[Beyond Reach]]. **Breachstones**, hatched at the Genesis Tree, reveal a Breach Domain on the Atlas with the Breachlords Tul and Esh in its fortress.
- **Lineage Support gems** such as [[Xoph's Pyre]], [[Tul's Stillness]] and [[Esh's Radiance]].

**Making currency.**

- Sell Catalysts in bulk on the Currency Exchange: every Breach drops several and crafters always need them.
- Breach Splinters and Breachstones sell to players who farm the bosses. If you do not fight Xesht yourself, sell them as you go.
- Use the Genesis Tree to target sought-after jewellery (minion or caster rings, the exclusive bases) and sell the good rolls. Catalysing a good ring before listing it raises its price.
- Lineage Support gems and Xesht's uniques are the jackpots.

**Best for** builds with fast, wide clear that keep up with swarms coming from every side. Minion and totem builds do well in Hives, where you fight from a fixed spot.

### Ritual

**How it works.** A Ritual map has three or four Ritual sites. Clear the monsters inside a circle, then activate the altar: the circle closes and every monster that died in it comes back. Kill them again to earn **Tribute**. Tribute does not carry over to the next map, so spend it before you leave.

**What you get and what it is for.** Tribute buys rewards from the Ritual page, made of uniques and **Omens**. Omens sit in your inventory and change the next use of a crafting currency: [[Omen of Greater Exaltation]] makes the next Exalted Orb add two modifiers, [[Omen of Dextral Annulment]] makes the next Orb of Annulment remove only a suffix. They are how expensive items get crafted.

You can spend Tribute four ways: take an item, **defer** it (pay a little to lock it, and it comes back cheaper in a later Ritual), **reroll** the page, or **offer** the rest to the King in the Mist. Filling his bar gives An Audience with the King: find the Crux of Nothingness on the Atlas to fight him. His Head starts the **Rite of the Nameless** at Caer Tarth: a chain of maps with guaranteed Rituals where you choose a reward for each map, currency included (Divine Orbs, or even a [[Mageblood]] with the right Atlas passive). Each map of the chain drops a Call of the Shadows; five of them open the fight with the pinnacle boss, The Bodach.

**Making currency.**

- Always take the Omens that sell for a lot: [[Omen of Sinistral Annulment]], [[Omen of Dextral Annulment]], [[Omen of Sinistral Erasure]], [[Omen of Dextral Erasure]], [[Omen of Whittling]] and [[Omen of Chance]].
- Skip cheap items on the first pages and reroll to see more of them. Keep 1,000 to 1,500 Tribute after your last reroll so you can defer a rare item if one shows up.
- Only take uniques you can use or sell: the rest of the Tribute is worth more as offerings to the King.
- An Audience with the King, the Head of the King and Call of the Shadows can all be sold if you do not want to fight.
- Ritual scales with the number of monsters in the map: pack size on Waystones and Tablets, Distilled Emotions, and Delirium, whose monsters (including its bosses) are revived by the altars too.

**Best for** builds with big area damage and the defences to hold their ground: the circle locks you in. Minions, totems and channelled skills are at home here, since standing still costs nothing.

### Delirium

**How it works.** Walk through the mirror at the start of the map and a fog spreads forward across it. The fog adds Delirium monsters and makes every monster tougher and more rewarding the further it is from the mirror. Keep up: spending more than four seconds outside the fog ends the encounter. Fracturing Mirrors along the way release extra packs, and the progress bar shows what is left before the boss: extra Rares, the unique bosses Kosis and Omniphobia, and the Loathsome Mire, a side area that ends with an amulet carrying two instilled notables.

The questline starts at the Withered Willow. Each map around it counts once you kill its boss under the effect of the mirror, gives a Delirium Atlas point and raises the area's deliriousness. At 100% a **Simulacrum** appears: waves of increasingly hard monsters, each with random negative modifiers, and Kosis at the top. It drops Raven's Reflection, which opens the pinnacle fight with Tang'Mazu.

**What you get and what it is for.**

- **Distilled Emotions** are spent at the Withered Willow. On an amulet, a combination of emotions instills a passive notable, often one your class cannot reach or one that saves several points. On a jewel they replace a modifier with a guaranteed one. On a Waystone they add extra modifiers: three [[Liquid Paranoia]] add about 36% more Rare monsters, and Rares drop most of the currency. Three emotions of the same kind combine into the next tier at the Reforging Bench.
- **Simulacrum Splinters** (with the right Atlas passive): 300 make a Simulacrum.
- Tang'Mazu drops [[Veilpiercer]], [[Sadist's Mercy]], [[The Auspex]], [[Horror's Flight]], The Raven's Flock and [[Split Personality]].

**Making currency.**

- Sell emotions on the Currency Exchange; combine three into the next tier when it is worth more than the three separately. The Atlas passive "You can't scare me anymore!" lets you drop Potent emotions, which are expensive.
- Sell splinters and Simulacrums to players who farm them, or run them yourself if your build handles the late waves.
- Check the Loathsome Mire amulets: two good notables on one amulet sell well.
- Spend your own Paranoia on Waystones: more Rares means more currency from every map.

**Best for** fast builds with strong clear that keep pace with the fog, and enough defences for the late Simulacrum waves. Slow or channelled builds struggle.

### Expedition

**How it works.** Place a fuse line of explosives (the button left of the skill bar), then hit the detonator. Everything inside the blast radius is unearthed: **Runic monsters** (red flags), **Remnants** (floating rocks) and chests. A Remnant has three to ten runes; each rune adds a wave of monsters, and higher rune counts offer better loot. Click a Remnant first to pick which reward it gives. Remnant modifiers make the monsters much more dangerous, so read them.

The Ruins of Kingsmarch, on the coast south-east of the Atlas start, lead to the ocean. **Logbooks** reveal new islands; Grand Expeditions (tier 6+, jagged edges) are one huge dig site with fifteen explosives, with no monsters until you unearth them and sometimes Mysterious Chests full of a single reward type. Bosses (Medved, Vorana, Uhtred, Olroth, and Styrn in a hidden tomb) lead to the Shattered Triskelion and the pinnacle fight with The Aberration.

**What you get and what it is for.** Runes, Verisium, Alloys and Fluxes for Runeforging and Ward, Kalguuran skill gems, Sagas (they force a given boss to appear in your next Logbook) and Crests. Remnants can also offer currency (up to ten Divine Orbs at once) or, once, a permanent character bonus: **+1 passive skill point**, +10 Spirit or +5% to elemental resistances. Expedition is the only endgame content that can give an extra passive point.

**Making currency.**

- Pick currency on Remnants when nothing better is offered, and favour Remnants with many runes and purple pre-socketed runes.
- Sell Logbooks, Sagas, Alloys, Fluxes and Verisium in bulk.
- In a Grand Expedition without a good Remnant, detonate one explosive near the start and move on: it is not worth the time.
- Rare chests and Runic monsters in normal maps also drop Logbooks: unearth them on the way.

**Best for** builds with solid defences and good damage on Rares: the best rewards come with the most dangerous Remnants. Builds that love large packs can let everything unearth first, then clear it in one go.

### Abyss

**How it works.** Green fissures in a map lead to a large Abyssal Pit. Kill monsters near the fissures to close them; each one spawns Abyssal monsters based on what you killed (normal, magic, Rares that keep their modifiers and can gain deadly Abyssal modifiers, Rogue Exiles that become Lichborn, bosses that summon a huge creature). Once every fissure is closed, the pit opens; clear it and an **Abyssal Trove** rises with a large amount of loot. On the Atlas, cracks spread from the Well of Souls in the east: those maps give Abyss Atlas points and end in Abyssal Depths with one of the Lightless bosses.

**What you get and what it is for.**

- **Bones** ([[Gnawed Jawbone]], [[Ancient Jawbone]] and the rib and collarbone versions) **desecrate** an item: they add a hidden modifier that you reveal at the Well of Souls, choosing one of three. It is a controlled way to add a strong modifier to an almost finished item.
- **Abyss Omens** steer desecration: [[Omen of Abyssal Echoes]] rerolls the three choices, [[Omen of Light]] removes a desecrated modifier so you can try again, [[Omen of Sinistral Necromancy]] and [[Omen of Dextral Necromancy]] force a prefix or a suffix, and the faction Omens force the modifiers only desecration can add.
- Sealing a pit can reveal a **Dark Domain**: an Abyssal City where every Rare has Abyssal modifiers, with exclusive Lineage Support gems such as [[Kurgal's Leash]], Omens of Abyssal Echoes and socketables. Other domains hold the Abyssal Commanders, which drop uniques such as [[Darkness Enthroned]] and [[Undying Hate]], and Kulemak's Invitation to the pinnacle fight with the Vessel of Kulemak.

**Making currency.**

- Sell bones in bulk: anyone crafting endgame gear needs them.
- Sell the Omens, especially Omens of Abyssal Echoes: players use them on every expensive desecration.
- Desecrate good bases yourself and sell the result when you hit a strong modifier.
- Lichborn exiles drop the sought-after Heart of the Well jewel; Kulemak's Invitation sells to bossers.

**Best for** builds with fast clear that also kill Rares quickly: pits are hordes of monsters, but the money is in the Rares with Abyssal modifiers.

### Vaal Temple

**How it works.** Kill the corrupted monsters in the Vaal circles that appear in maps to collect Energized Crystals; six open a portal to the Vaal ruins. The Lira Vaal city, north on the Atlas, has guaranteed beacons and gives Fate of the Vaal Atlas points. In the temple console you place six rooms per run on an 80-square board and connect them. Rooms upgrade each other when placed next to the right neighbour (hold ALT on a room to see which), up to tier 3: higher tiers are harder and pay more. After each run part of the temple **destabilises** and disappears, much more after killing the Architect or Atziri.

**What you get and what it is for.**

- **Room bonuses** stack over the whole temple: Alchemy Labs and Smithies raise item rarity, Garrisons and Commanders add and strengthen monsters, Synthflesh Labs raise experience. A tier 3 Flesh Surgeon replaces one of your limbs with a mechanical one, an extra modifier until you die.
- **Vaal crafting** in the best rooms: corrupting an already corrupted item a second time, ancient Soul Cores from Vaal Constructs, and special affixes on corrupted Vaal uniques.
- **Restricted rooms**, unlocked by beating the Architect, are one-time vaults: currency (Kishara's Vault), a Lineage Support (Vault of Reverence), a rune, Precursor Tablets, a unique, or a bench that destroys an item and returns its socketed rune. The Royal Access Chamber opens the path to Atziri.
- **Medallions** shape the board: Juatalotli's locks a room against destabilisation (the most important one), Quipolatl's upgrades a room, others reroll or add rooms.

**Making currency.**

- Build a long chain of rooms (a "snake") around rarity and monster rooms, and lock its end with a Juatalotli's Medallion so the whole chain survives destabilisation.
- Place the Architect early in a good spot, then skip him and Atziri while the temple is growing: both wipe large parts of it.
- Sell what the vaults give: Lineage Support gems, uniques, Tablets and Soul Cores.
- Double corruption is a gamble: use it on items you can afford to lose, or on cheap uniques that become valuable with the right outcome.

**Best for** sturdy builds that kill both packs and bosses: upgraded rooms raise monster power across the board, and the Architect and Atziri are real boss fights. It also asks for planning more than any other mechanic.

### Trial of the Sekhemas

**How it works.** A **Djinn Barya** (dropped by monsters from Act 2, higher level ones give more floors) opens the trial. Place Relics on the Relic Altar, then go through four floors of seven rooms with a boss at the end of each. **Honour** is the trial's own health bar: it equals your life plus energy shield, every hit removes some, and the run ends at zero. Armour reduces the Honour lost per hit, and evasion gives a chance to lose none. Afflictions make the run harder, Boons easier; Sacred Water buys Boons and Relics from merchants.

**What you get and what it is for.**

- **Keys** (bronze, silver, gold) open the reward chests at the end of each floor. Chest types, best first: Spectrum (unique [[Grand Spectrum]] jewels), Time-Lost (Time-Lost Jewels, found only here, which strengthen the passives around their socket), Royal (jewels), Arcanist's (currency), Relic, then random items and equipment.
- **Relics** are permanent upgrades for future runs: more Honour, Honour resistance (aim for 75% in total), more keys or relics, extra rooms revealed.
- Zarokh, the Temporal, at the end of floor four, drops unique Relics that turn the next run into a challenge (no Honour recovery, one Honour…) with an extra reward, including exclusive uniques.

**Making currency.**

- Take Key rooms first; spend leftover Sacred Water on extra keys at the Keth Forge before the end.
- Jewels are the bulk of the profit: Time-Lost and Grand Spectrum jewels sell well, so open those chests first.
- Relics with Honour resistance and increased relic quantity sell for a lot; keep the rest on your altar.
- Djinn Barya sell to players who need their ascendancy points, and so do trial carries.

**Best for** builds that avoid getting hit: ranged characters, minions and totems that keep enemies off you, with a large life or energy shield pool. Melee builds lose Honour much faster.

### Trial of Chaos

**How it works.** An **Inscribed Ultimatum** (from Act 3, area level 75+ for the full ten rounds) opens the trial. Before each round, pick one of three negative modifiers; they stay for the whole run and many can be upgraded. Rounds are short challenges (survive, kill, defend, sacrifice monsters in blood circles…), and rounds 4, 7 and 10 are boss fights. Rewards pile up as you go, but you lose all of them if you die. After round 10 you can stop or wager your rewards to go on, up to 30 rounds.

**What you get and what it is for.**

- **Chaos and Vaal Orbs** from monsters, currency from every normal round and a reward chest at the end of each round.
- **Soul Cores** from rounds 4, 7 and 10, socketed in your gear instead of runes for strong modifiers. [[Soul Core of Azcapa]] and [[Soul Core of Quipolatl]] are the most valuable.
- **Fates** from the round 10 bosses: the three of them open the Trialmaster's tower, whose drops include [[Glimpse of Chaos]], [[Zerphi's Genesis]], [[Hateforge]] and [[The Adorned]]. He sometimes drops corrupted Ultimatums that double a sacrifice (10 Vaal Orbs in, 20 out).

**Making currency.**

- Pick modifiers your build ignores. Chaos Inoculation ignores the chaos damage one; physical damage ignores the enemy resistance one; energy shield builds must never take the one that lowers energy shield, armour and evasion. Avoid the recovery, petrification and boss modifiers unless you kill bosses in seconds.
- Stop the run when you hold a valuable reward (an Azcapa or Quipolatl core, a Divine Orb) and the next modifiers look dangerous.
- Wager of the Future, late in a run, doubles the stack size of the currency still on offer: one of the best wagers. Lethal Rare Monsters adds Rares, and so loot, if you can handle them.
- Fates sell for a lot: unless you want the Trialmaster's uniques, selling them is the steadier profit.

**Best for** sturdy builds with strong boss damage: three of the ten rounds are boss fights, and the modifiers stack up round after round.

## Picking a Mechanic for Your Build

Play to your strengths. A build with great clear and modest single-target damage earns more from mechanics with many monsters (Breach, Ritual, Abyss, Delirium) than from bossing. A build that kills bosses fast but clears slowly is better off with pinnacle fights and boss carries. If you have fun with a mechanic you will run more maps, which matters more than small differences in reward.

To add a mechanic reliably, take its nodes on the Atlas passive tree as you unlock them, then use its Precursor Tablets on your maps, in the Map Device or in a Lost Tower that covers several uncleared maps. Run your best Waystones on the affected maps.

> **League mechanic.** Each league adds its own mechanic and changes the economy. Early in a league it is often the most profitable; check what its rewards sell for before committing.

## Selling: the Habits That Matter

**Start with a loot filter.** Before you can sell items you have to pick them up. A good filter highlights what has value and hides the rest, so you spend less time looking at the floor and more time killing monsters.

**Price check stackables once.** For stackable currency, use the Currency Exchange (Alva, in the Ziggurat Refuge or your hideout): put Exalted Orb on the "I want" side and your item on the "I have" side to see what buyers pay, then place an order for a small gold fee. Check these at least once to learn what your maps really drop: Waystones, Jeweller's Orbs of every size, Gemcutter's Prisms, Distilled Emotions, Essences, Expedition currency, Catalysts and Breachstones. Leaving them in your stash is the most common beginner mistake.

**Dump tab the rest.** Set a premium stash tab to a fixed price, for example 2 Exalted Orbs, and fill it with anything decent. Items that look worthless sell more often than you would expect. Selling now at half its value beats never selling it, and if several people whisper you at once for the same item, it is underpriced: check it on the trade site and relist it. Do not overcorrect, though: the gap between being flooded with whispers and getting none can be a single Exalted Orb.

**Price check rares only when they get attention.** On the trade site, pick the item category (some bases, like Ruby Rings, are worth more), enter its modifiers with slightly lower minimums, and search. If nothing matches exactly, untick modifiers and search one to three at a time to find which combination buyers want.

**Sell while you play.** Talk to Ange in Kingsmarch (Act 4) to bring her to your hideout, then use Manage Shop to list items. Buyers on the trade site get a "Secure Item" button, travel to your hideout and buy from her, even when you are offline. Premium tabs bought before patch 0.3.0 can be converted to merchant tabs from your account page on pathofexile2.com.

## Spending Less Time in Your Hideout

Income is time spent multiplied by efficiency. If you spend more time sorting, pricing and listing than mapping, cut back: kill more monsters instead. And keep it fun: a build you enjoy runs more maps than an optimal one you avoid logging into.

## More Ways to Make Currency

- **Craft good bases.** In higher tier maps, run Transmutation, Augmentation and Regal Orbs on the valuable bases your filter highlights. Below the "Expert" bases it is rarely worth it, except for jewellery. Skip the Regal if the first two modifiers are bad.
- **Craft for profit.** Good items are always in demand. Sometimes it is as simple as buying a magic ring with one resistance and using an Essence to guarantee a second one.
- **Flip items.** Buy underpriced items and relist them at their real value.
- **Sell services.** Boss carries, ascendancy trial completions or party slots in sought-after maps.

Test the demand first with small amounts before committing to any of these.
