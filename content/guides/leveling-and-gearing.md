Path of Exile 2 gives you a lot of systems at once: gems, passives, runes, a dozen currencies, crafting benches. You do not need to master them to have fun. This guide covers what actually moves the needle, in the order you meet it.

> **The short version.** Upgrade your movement speed boots and flasks as soon as you can, keep your resistances as high as possible, level your main skill gem, and do not judge an item by its colour: judge it by its stats.

## Before You Start: Pick a Build

The single biggest time saver is following a build that is known to work. Builds rated well in the [League Start tier list](/tier-list) are designed to work with whatever drops in the campaign and do not need uniques. Each guide on this site lists its skills, its ascendancy order and the gear stages from campaign to endgame.

You can still experiment: the goal is to have a plan for your main skill and your defences, not to copy every passive point.

## Leveling Through the Campaign

### Five rules to stop dying

Most campaign deaths come from the same three things: fighting in a bad position, poor flask use, and low life or resistances. These habits fix most of them:

1. **Keep moving.** Standing still is how ranged attacks and slams find you.
2. **Run in circles, not straight lines.** Moving around a threat dodges more than running away from it.
3. **Stay close to bosses.** Many boss attacks are cones or follow you; next to the boss, three steps get you out, while at range you need thirty.
4. **Drink before you need it.** Press your life flask as a big hit is coming, not after it lands.
5. **Dodge roll.** It also makes you smaller, so you can slip between monsters that block your path.

### Take the free permanent stats

The campaign hides permanent rewards in optional content: extra passive points (24 across the acts and interludes), +10% to all elemental resistances from Acts 1 to 3, and up to 100 Spirit from optional bosses. Spirit is what lets you run auras, heralds and minions, so do not skip those fights. Maxroll's page on [permanent stats](https://maxroll.gg/poe2/getting-started/permanent-stats-from-campaign) lists where each one is.

### Keep your main skill gem up to date

Your damage mostly comes from the level of your main skill. Use new Uncut Skill Gems on the skills that deal your damage first, and use a Gemcutter's Prism on skills you will keep: adding quality to a gem has no downside.

### Solve attribute and mana problems early

- **Can't equip an item?** The small attribute nodes on the passive tree can be respecced for a little gold, and amulets like Amber, Jade or Lapis give attributes on their own.
- **Always out of mana?** Upgrade your mana flask (it is your strongest tool), look for "gains charges per second" on it, and check whether a support gem is raising your costs without adding much. A single "mana on kill" modifier on a ring can fix clearing almost by itself.

## What to Look For on Gear While Leveling

In rough order of importance:

1. **Movement speed** on boots.
2. **Resistances.**
3. **Life** (Strength also gives life).
4. **Base damage:** a better weapon and flat added damage for attacks, +skill levels for spells.
5. **Damage scaling:** attack or cast speed, "increased" damage of your type.
6. **Armour, evasion or energy shield**, depending on your build.

> **Rarity is not power.** A magic (blue) item with life, a resistance and good armour often beats a rare (yellow) item with random stats. Rares only have more *potential*; compare the actual numbers.

```items
cards:
  - base: Spired Greathelm
    rarity: magic
    mods: [+52 to maximum Life, +18% to Cold Resistance]
    verdict: good
    note: "Keep this one: life and a resistance you need."
  - base: Spired Greathelm
    rarity: rare
    mods: [+12 to maximum Mana, +7 to Intelligence, 1.4 Life Regeneration per second, 5% increased Light Radius, +14 to Accuracy Rating]
    verdict: bad
    note: "More modifiers, but none of them keeps you alive."
```


### Upgrade timeline

These are the moments when a meaningful upgrade becomes available. Check drops and vendors around these levels.

| Level | Upgrade |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Boots with 10% movement speed |
| 4 | Medium flasks (free from the cauldron in the Grelwood witch hut) |
| 8 | Ruby ring, attribute amulets, flasks that gain charges per second, Stone Charm against stuns |
| 10 | Greater flasks (buy them in town if they have not dropped) |
| 12 | Sapphire ring: have some cold resistance before the end of Act 1 |
| 16 | 15% movement speed boots, Grand flasks, Topaz ring |
| 23 | Giant flasks, belts with an extra charm slot |
| 30 | Colossal flasks, Solar Amulet (an extra support for a Spirit skill) |
| 33 | 20% movement speed boots |
| 35 | Prismatic rings (all elemental resistances) |
| 40 | Gargantuan flasks |
| 46 | 25% movement speed boots: worth a Regal Orb if they also have a resistance |
| 50 / 60 | Transcendent, then Ultimate flasks: spend currency to get good modifiers, they carry you into maps |

### Use the town tools

- **Vendors** restock as you progress. Each time you are in town, check them against your most pressing problem: a missing resistance, old flasks, no movement speed, a weapon two acts behind.
- **The gambler** sells a random item of the type you pick. Expensive in gold, but the fastest way to get a specific weapon type you are missing.
- **Runes** are your best campaign crafting tool. Socket damage runes in your weapon, and resistance runes in armour to fix a weak resistance before a hard boss. Salvage gear with quality or sockets you will not use: it gives you the currency to add sockets and quality.
- **The reforging bench** (from Act 3) turns three failed copies of the same base and rarity into a new one. Useful when you need one specific base, like a jewellery type or a key weapon.
- **Disenchant or sell?** Sell early for gold (flasks, weapons, jewellery from vendors). Then disenchant magic and rare items to stock up on Transmutation and Regal shards for crafting. Balance the two based on what you need.

## Spending Currency in the Campaign

| Currency | Use it on |
| --- | --- |
| Orb of Transmutation | Any white item worth turning blue: new weapon bases, boots, gloves, flasks, jewellery |
| Orb of Augmentation | A blue item with one good modifier and a free slot |
| Regal Orb | Your best blue items with two good modifiers that you will keep a while (not boots you replace in two levels) |
| Orb of Alchemy | Rare; keep for a weapon close to your level or a Solar Amulet |
| Essences | Guarantee one modifier: damage on a new weapon, attributes on jewellery, movement speed on boots |
| Gemcutter's Prism | Your long-term skill gems, any time |
| Whetstone / Armourer's Scrap | Only on items you keep for long, like a good attack weapon from level 30 |

> **Trade league tip.** Do not spend Exalted, Chaos or Divine Orbs during the campaign. They are worth far more as trade currency: a couple of Exalted Orbs can buy a weapon that doubles your damage, or a strong leveling unique.

## Arriving in Maps: The Checklist

Your gear will be a patchwork of campaign items, and that is normal. Before pushing harder maps, fix these in order:

1. **75% fire, cold and lightning resistance.** Map damage is balanced around capped resistances. At 50% instead of 75% you take *double* the damage, not a little more. Chaos resistance is welcome but not required at first.
2. **25-30% movement speed boots.**
3. **A weapon that matches your level.** For attacks, the weapon is most of your damage.
4. **Good flasks** with charge regeneration.
5. **Extra links** for your main skill with Jeweller's Orbs.

### Trading

You can trade almost everything:

- **The Currency Exchange** (from Alva in your hideout or the refuge) swaps stackable items, like orbs and essences, through buy and sell orders.
- **Asynchronous trade:** put items in a Merchant's Tab with a price and other players can buy them while you are offline. You collect your earnings later.
- **The trade website** (or the `/` key in game) searches every listed item. Always set your item level and the modifiers you really need; being too specific makes everything expensive.

Most of your income comes from ordinary drops: waystones, Jeweller's Orbs, Gemcutter's Prisms, essences, distilled emotions. Price them once so you know what is worth picking up, and list them instead of leaving them in your stash.

### Use a loot filter

A loot filter hides junk and highlights what matters for your stage of the game. NeverSink's filters are the standard; start on "Semi-Strict" and move to "Strict" once you are settled in maps.

## Improving Your Gear in the Endgame

### Read an item

- **Prefixes and suffixes.** Magic items have up to one of each, rares up to three of each. Each item type has its own pool: movement speed on boots is a prefix, so boots with three prefixes can never gain it.
- **Item level** decides how strong the rolled modifiers can be. You only need the item level of the best tier you are aiming for, and lower item levels are much cheaper.
- **Base type** sets the defence type and attribute requirements. A slightly worse base is often far cheaper and almost as good.

Sites like PoE2DB and Craft of Exile list every modifier an item can roll and the item level each tier needs.

### Beginner-friendly crafting

Start from a white or magic item of the right base and item level, ideally with one or two modifiers you want already on it. From there:

- **Greater Essences** turn a good magic item into a rare with a guaranteed third modifier.
- **Greater and Perfect orbs** raise the minimum strength of what they add.
- **Omens** make currencies more predictable: pick prefix or suffix, double an Exalted Orb, or control what a Chaos Orb removes.
- **Desecration** (Abyss currencies) adds a modifier and lets you choose between three options: a good way to finish an item or save a near miss.
- **Fluxes** swap the resistance on an item to another element. Keep this in mind when trading: an item with the "wrong" resistance is often cheaper.
- **Vaal Orbs** corrupt an item permanently and can ruin it. Only use them on finished items.

An item can only hold **one** crafted modifier (from an essence or similar). Plan around it.

### Damage, in one paragraph

Look at the **tags** under your skill's name: they tell you what scales it. **Attacks** take most of their damage from your weapon, so weapon upgrades and flat added damage come first, then attack speed. **Spells** take their base damage from the gem level, so +skill levels, cast speed and spell damage matter most. "Increased" bonuses add together; "more" bonuses multiply, which is why support gems are so strong.

## Common Mistakes

- Keeping a rare or unique that has worse stats than a magic item in your bag.
- Ignoring resistances until you start dying.
- Fighting the Act 1 final boss with almost no cold resistance.
- Spending Exalted Orbs on campaign gear in a trade league.
- Leaving currency and sellable items sitting in the stash.
- Linking support gems that raise mana cost without adding much.
